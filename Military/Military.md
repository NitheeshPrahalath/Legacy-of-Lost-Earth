Everything belongs to one institutional design language.

Its visual philosophy is:

* severe
* institutional
* architectural
* militaristic
* technologically sophisticated
* standardized
* durable
* precise
* restrained
* intimidating

**geometry + materials + standardization + hierarchy.**

# LVI. CLEARANCE SYSTEM

Clearance is independent of:

* rank
* branch
* unit
* seniority

The fundamental rule is:

> **Rank determines authority. Clearance determines information and system access.**

A high-ranking officer does not automatically receive every clearance.

A lower-ranking specialist may receive a higher clearance if their assignment requires it.

---

# LVII. CLEARANCE LEVELS

The numbering runs in the opposite direction from conventional intuitive numbering.

## LEVEL 5 — GENERAL

The ordinary baseline.

Access includes:

* general military regulations
* routine organizational information
* ordinary communications
* basic logistics
* standard personnel systems
* non-sensitive operational information

**Level 5 is the normal clearance level.**

---

## LEVEL 4 — RESTRICTED

For personnel requiring limited sensitive information.

Access includes:

* unit-sensitive information
* restricted logistics
* protected communications
* local facility information
* limited operational intelligence

---

## LEVEL 3 — OPERATIONAL

For personnel with meaningful operational responsibilities.

Access includes:

* tactical intelligence
* regional operations
* protected operational networks
* deployment information
* sensitive logistics
* multi-unit coordination

---

## LEVEL 2 — COMMAND

For senior command, intelligence, strategic planning, and specialized technical personnel.

Access includes:

* strategic military intelligence
* multi-regional operations
* protected command networks
* advanced systems information
* sensitive strategic logistics
* cross-branch operational information

---

# LVIII. LEVEL 1 — SOVEREIGN

Level 1 is the **highest clearance known to ordinary SDD personnel**.

Personnel are taught that Level 1 represents the highest possible clearance.

Access can include:

* national strategic intelligence
* supreme-level defense planning
* strategic weapons information
* national defense infrastructure
* highest protected communications
* extremely restricted command systems

But Level 1 personnel do not necessarily know that another level exists.

---

# LIX. LEVEL 0 — ABSOLUTE

## CLASSIFICATION: EXISTENCE RESTRICTED

Level 0 is the true highest clearance.

Its existence is itself classified.

Ordinary personnel do not know that Level 0 exists.

Therefore, the normal SDD hierarchy taught to personnel is:

**Level 5 → Level 4 → Level 3 → Level 2 → Level 1**

with:

> **Level 1 = highest known clearance**

The actual system is:

**Level 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0**

with:

> **Level 0 = absolute clearance**

The contents of Level 0 should remain extremely restricted.

It can govern access to the deepest strategic architecture of the SDD, systems above conventional branch authority, and information whose existence itself is compartmentalized.

---

# LX. CLEARANCE IS NOT RANK

This distinction must be explicit.

For example:

| Personnel               | Rank             | Clearance |
| ----------------------- | ---------------- | --------- |
| Field Officer           | Captain-Prime    | Level 4   |
| Battalion Commander     | Commander-Regent | Level 3   |
| Intelligence Specialist | Major-Tactical   | Level 2   |
| Regional Commander      | Theater-General  | Level 1   |
| Technical Specialist    | Sub-Prime        | Level 2   |

These are not contradictions.

The person's **position determines the clearance requirement**.

Therefore:

> **Rank does not automatically grant clearance.**

---

# LXI. CLEARANCE COMPARTMENTS

Clearance also has a second dimension.

A person receives:

**CLEARANCE LEVEL + ACCESS COMPARTMENT**

Examples:

**LEVEL 3 — TPV**

or:

**LEVEL 3 — HRA / OSL / JOINT**

or:

**LEVEL 1 — STRATEGIC / JOINT**

This prevents a person from automatically accessing every subject merely because they possess a high clearance.

---

# LXII. THREE-LAYER ACCESS MODEL

The final security model is:

### 1. CLEARANCE LEVEL

**How sensitive is the information?**

### 2. COMPARTMENT

**What subject or domain does it belong to?**

### 3. AUTHORIZATION

**Does this person have permission to access this particular system or facility?**

Therefore:

> **Clearance Level = depth**

> **Compartment = domain**

> **Authorization = specific permission**

A Level 1 HRA officer does not automatically receive access to Level 1 OSL information.

---
