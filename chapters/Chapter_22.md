# Chapter 22

**Mountain Foothills base Town, Kyoto \| October 15, 2120 \| 14:30**

"Alright. Iris, can you help me find a way underground?"

"Let me check for mutated animal activity in the surrounding area. My sensors aren't able to accurately trace activity because of the number of corpses and disturbances. My best guess is that the location with the highest concentration of activity will have the highest probability of containing an underground structure."

Kael frowned.

"Iris... that's a pretty crude method."

"I am aware."

Kael sighed.

"But I can't think of another way either."

Following Iris's instructions, Kael moved through the abandoned buildings, carefully making his way deeper into the town.

Along the way, they encountered several mutated creatures.

Mutated crows descended from rooftops.

Mutated insects swarmed through broken windows and ventilation shafts.

Kael and Cherry dealt with them quickly.

None of the creatures posed a serious threat individually, but their constant attacks made their search more difficult.

A few minutes later, they reached the edge of a wide clearing.

Kael stopped.

His eyes widened.

There it was.

The entrance to the underground shelter was a massive circular dome, with nearly half of its structure buried beneath the ground. The exposed portion was reinforced with thick metal plating.

But the most surprising part wasn't the shelter itself.

It was what was happening around it.

"Those are..."

Kael stared.

SDD personnel.

Fifteen soldiers in heavy combat suits stood in a defensive line around the partially closed entrance, which appeared to be experiencing some kind of malfunction.

But they weren't using energy weapons.

Instead, the soldiers were armed with crude-looking mechanical weapons.

Wind-up crossbow repeaters fired bolts from behind barricades.

Spring-piston air rifles sent quiet, precise shots through narrow gaps.

Several soldiers threw heavy harpoons, anchoring them into the ground or into the bodies of larger creatures attempting to break through the defensive line.

Nearby, a handful of autonomous quadruped weapon platforms sat completely motionless.

Powerless.

The soldiers had also positioned two layers of High Mobility Military Vehicles around the entrance, creating a defensive perimeter against the approaching creatures.

Kael's expression became serious.

The reason for the primitive weapons was immediately obvious.

Their energy supply had run dry.

For the past month, there had been no functioning power facility in the area. Every pulse rifle, electric baton, and capacitor cell had eventually become useless.

Nothing remained to recharge them.

The SDD personnel had been cut off from the grid.

So they had returned to weapons that didn't need electricity.

Mechanical weapons.

Weapons that could still function when everything else had gone dark.

But despite their preparations, the situation was desperate.

The shelter was surrounded.

More than thirty mutated animals crowded around the defensive perimeter.

Some were noticeably larger and stronger than the others.

Kael recognized several species.

Mutated Japanese wild boars.

Mutated sika deer.

Mutated Japanese serows.

Mutated raccoon dogs.

Mutated red foxes.

Mutated large-billed crows.

Mutated monkeys.

Mutated raccoons.

Mutated cats.

Mutated dogs.

And there were even more creatures mixed into the swarm.

The animals weren't attacking randomly.

They were surrounding the shelter from every direction, constantly searching for a weakness in the SDD defenses.

Every time one creature charged forward, the soldiers focused their fire on it.

Bolts struck flesh.

Air rifles cracked quietly.

Harpoons flew through the air.

Some creatures fell.

Others simply became more aggressive.

Kael watched the battlefield.

He felt an urge to launch himself into the fight.

But he controlled himself.

He had learned enough during his month on Mount Myōken to know that charging blindly into a battle was no longer acceptable.

He needed to understand the situation first.

"Iris."

"Yes?"

"Tell me which ones are the strongest and weakest. And tell me if I can handle this group."

Iris remained silent for a few seconds as she analyzed the creatures.

"Based on their physical appearances and movement patterns, most of them appear to be approximately eight to nine times stronger than an average human."

Kael's eyes narrowed.

"And the strongest?"

"The largest specimens are significantly more powerful. I estimate approximately eleven to twelve times the strength of an average human."

Kael studied the battlefield.

Iris continued.

"If you cooperate with the SDD personnel, your chances of successfully handling this group will increase considerably."

Kael nodded slowly.

"Which ones should I deal with first?"

"I recommend the Japanese wild boar and the monkey."

"Why?"

"The wild boar possesses the greatest raw physical power among the creatures present. The monkey is considerably more mobile and capable of rapidly changing its attack direction."

Kael focused his eyes on them.

The mutated wild boar was enormous.

Its body was covered in thick, coarse hair, and its tusks had grown into frightening weapons.

Beside it, the mutated monkey moved constantly, jumping between vehicles and trees while searching for an opening.

Kael took a slow breath.

"Alright."

He lowered his stance.

"I'll deal with the Boar."

Cherry stepped forward beside him.

Kael glanced at her.

"You take the Monkey."

Cherry's ears twitched.

She looked toward the battlefield.

Then toward Kael.

She seemed to understand exactly what he meant.

Kael looked back at the SDD soldiers.

He and Cherry rounded behind the defensive line as slowly as possible—weaving between ruined vehicles and collapsed walls, keeping low in the shadow of the barricades. For a few heartbeats, it seemed they would remain unseen.

Then the wild boar swung its massive head toward the rubble where Kael was crouched.

It charged.

Kael exploded out of cover. His spear slammed into the boar's shoulder; flesh tore and blood spurted out. It lowered its head and carved a trench through the earth with its tusks, forcing him sideways before he could recover.

Behind the barricades, one of the defenders froze.

"Who the hell is that?"

A civilian had just met the boar head-on, held its charge, drawn blood. Half the squad forgot their reloads, staring at the ragged teenager who had done in one blow what their harpoons had failed to do in an hour.

But Kael couldn't finish it.

Two mutated raccoon dogs burst from the underbrush and threw themselves at him, snapping at his legs. He drove the butt of his spear into one's skull, spun to catch the other with a kick—and the boar's head caught him square in the ribs, sending him rolling through the rubble. Before he could rise, a mutated red fox slipped in low, jaws reaching for his throat. Kael barely got his forearm across its neck in time, his free hand clawing for the spear as the boar wheeled around for another charge.

He was being ground down from every direction.

Across the clearing, Cherry was a white blur.

The mutated monkey launched itself from a vehicle rooftop, claws spread mid-air—and never reached the ground. Cherry collided with it in a burst of fur and blood, teeth locking onto its throat as the two rolled through the dirt.

The SDD line found its second wind. Bolts and quiet air-rifle cracks hammered the creatures harrying Kael; one raccoon dog crumpled mid-lunge, another staggered back with an air-rifle bolt through its eye. Freed from the swarm, Kael rolled clear, planted his feet, and drove his spear between the boar's ribs. The boar screamed, bucked, and fought back—it took nearly ten minutes of clawing, dodging, and trading blows before it folded, his spear finally punching through the soft tissue behind its foreleg.

Cherry finished the monkey in under five minutes and turned on the rest of the swarm.

Forty minutes after the first shot, the last mutated creature fell.

Silence settled over the clearing.

The fight was over.

But the SDD soldiers did not lower their weapons.

The fifteen of them stood frozen behind their barricades, crossbows still trained on the place where Kael and Cherry stood. They weren't aiming at him—not yet. They simply didn't know what they were looking at.

An unknown civilian.

Unknown capabilities.

A monster they'd been fighting for an hour had just been torn apart in front of them.

"Everyone hold position!" a voice barked from the line. "Don't fire!"

One exhausted soldier stared at the collapsed boar. "He... he actually killed it."

"That thing almost wiped our squad," another answered. "And he took it alone."

"Hold your line!" the voice snapped. Then, directed at Kael: "Civilian! Identify yourself!"

Kael raised his open hands, spear held loose and pointing at the ground. "I came to help."

The tension didn't vanish.

Somewhere behind the barricades, a man muttered: "Whatever he is... he's damn strong."

Another, quieter still: "Is he even human?"

Kael waited.

Now that they were close, he could see them clearly.

Their armor was heavy and angular. Olive-gray plates sat over graphite-black combat uniforms, tied together by gunmetal protective components, matte black gloves, matte black boots. Broad shoulders and a dense, reinforced torso gave every soldier a silhouette that seemed to radiate mass—each one looked like a walking wall of plating, heavier than any other branch could manage. On the left chest of each uniform sat the standard SDD Diamond Parallax Badge, its top direction carrying the thin, machined etching of an armored chevron. The marking of the Territorial Pacification Vanguard.

TPV troops. The immovable shield of the Directorate's ground forces.

"Kael." Iris's voice entered his mind. "The TPV is one of the four public branches of the Sovereign Defense Directorate. The Territorial Pacification Vanguard serves as the ground military — army operations, armored warfare, and territorial defense. It is the heaviest of the four."

He watched as the soldiers began to move.

"The Hydro-Resource Armada (HRA) is the maritime branch," Iris continued, "responsible for naval warfare and the protection of coastal infrastructure. The Atmospheric Supremacy Command (ASC) controls the skies — air superiority, atmospheric defense, and strategic aviation. And the Orbital Strike & Logistics (OSL) division manages orbit itself, the satellite grid, ICBM defense, and long-range strategic support."

The commander stepped forward.

He did not hurry. He checked his fallen soldiers first—two pairs of hands helping a limping man toward the dome's entrance. Only when he was satisfied that his people were accounted for did he walk toward Kael, unhurried and unreadable.

The soldier in front stopped and removed his helmet.

"You have our thanks," he said. "You saved my people."

A brief pause.

"Who are you?"

"Kael Ardyn."

Then Cherry moved.

In a single silent bound she launched off the rubble and landed squarely on Kael's shoulder, tail curling as she surveyed the soldiers before her.

The line exploded.

"Mutated animal!"

Crossbows snapped up. Air rifles rose. Fifteen barrels trained on the small white shape that had just torn a monkey apart bare-clawed minutes earlier.

"Hold!" Kael shouted. "She's not a threat!"

The weapons didn't lower.

Kael felt Cherry's muscles coil beneath his palm.

"Cherry, no," he said quietly. "Stay."

A low growl rumbled in her chest, ears flattening as she met the guns with cold fury. Her claws dug into Kael's shoulder pad.

"Cherry." His voice softened, firm. "They're scared. That's all. Don't."

She held still a moment longer—then her ears twitched, and the tension bled out of her frame. She sat, watchful but calm.

"Put your weapons down," Kael said again, quieter now. "If I meant you harm, I wouldn't have saved your people."

The commander shook his head as if clearing his thoughts. "I don't trust you, nor your mutated cat, but it's true that you saved us. We will trust you for now."

He gestured toward his soldiers.

Slowly, one by one, the barrels lowered.

The commander studied him for a moment. "Where did you come from, and how are you able to fight like that?"

Kael hesitated—then, deciding trust would cost less than secrecy now, he spoke.

"I wasn't always here. I was in Osaka when the animals mutated and killed people. A mutated tree—something I still can't fully explain—took me and transported me to Mount Myōken in an instant." He let that settle. "For the past month, I traveled on foot through the entire forest, surviving everything that mountain threw at me. I came out of the mountain range and came across this town. I thought there was a good chance of SDD units here, so I came. As for how I'm so strong... I ate the meat of the mutated animals, and over time, I became stronger."

A long silence greeted him.

The commander looked around and spoke. "We were stationed at an underground base beneath Mount Myōken. When there was a blast from the south-southwest, a loud one, the ground shook for miles."

"Within a few minutes of the blast, the power died."

"Everything. Every electrical system in the base went dark at once. No emergency lights, no comms, no weapons. Right after that, every animal in the region went berserk. Ferocious. Aggressive. They started hunting us. With no way to defend ourselves and no way to recharge it, we had to ransack the entire underground base just to survive. That's where we found the High Mobility Vehicles—they were kept in a storage bay under the mountain. None of us knew how to ride them, so we ransacked the maintenance bay for a manual, studied it by flashlight, and used whatever fuel we recovered to escape the mountain range."

Kael listened.

"We made it down on those vehicles. Empty road, dead towns, all of it. Along the way, we saw what had happened to the other SDD units assigned to the mountain. They were all dead. Killed, along with most of the civilians. A few had made it out and were hiding."

"We reached this town because it had a military shelter—this dome. We rushed inside, pulled out the remaining civilians and surviving SDD personnel, and set up a guard at the entrance. It's been our home ever since."

He paused.

"The electrical equipment came back to life a few days later. But this shelter has no connection to any power grid—it threw its own breakers when the whole network collapsed, and there's no way to bring it back up. No way to recharge anything. And what little mechanical power we had left... it's almost gone."

The soldier in front stepped close.

He came to attention and saluted, fist to chest.

"Captain-Prime Yuto Ariga," he said. "Territorial Pacification Vanguard. Commander of the TPV contingent attached to Joint Base Twenty-Six, Himeji Naval Fortress, Western Defense Command."

Behind him, the other fourteen soldiers snapped the same salute. Each moved with the same clipped, standard discipline—commissioned Sub-Prime officers, the survivors of a command that had once been far larger.

He lowered his hand.

"If you two hadn't saved us, we might not have held this line."

"We should be safe for a while since we held off this wave, but soon other mutated animals will gather."
