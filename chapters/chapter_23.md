# Chapter 23

**Kyoto Shelter Complex, Mountain Foothills | October 15, 2120 | 15:40**

The dome's inner door groaned open.

A dim, amber-lit corridor stretched downward, descending into the earth at a sharp angle. The air was stale—warm, recycled, and heavy with the smell of bodies and old air filtration.

"Fifty-two meters," Iris said quietly. "Three floors."

Kael stepped inside alone, Cherry at his heels. Ariga led him down, the other fourteen soldiers fanning out to hold the entrance.

The first floor hit him like a wall.

Hundreds of civilians lay packed together across the reinforced floor, shoulder to shoulder in narrow rows. Makeshift bedding. Strips of torn uniform serving as bandages. Every few meters, someone was crying.

And every few meters, someone was dying.

An elderly man convulsed violently against the floor, body arching, teeth grinding, veins dark against his skin. A woman beside him held his head, screaming for help no doctor came to give. Further down the row, a young boy went quiet mid-cry—then began shaking, eyes rolled back, foam streaking his chin.

"Mutation illness," Ariga said, not breaking stride. "It doesn't come from the Beasts attacking. It comes from the air. From the water. Their bodies are changing faster than they can handle, and we have nothing to treat it."

Kael said nothing.

He forced himself to keep walking.

The second floor was worse in a different way.

It was quieter. The people here were too exhausted to cry. They waited, hollow-eyed, rationing nothing because there was nothing left to ration. Empty nutrient pods stacked in a corner. A dead man in the aisle that no one had moved yet. A mother shielding her child, watching Kael pass with the dull suspicion of someone who had been lied to for a month.

"How many?" Kael asked.

"Roughly three thousand civilians survived," Ariga answered. "That's it. Out of everything that was in this town. Three thousand, with no food, no medicine, and a dying shelter."

The third floor was sealed.

An armored hatch split the floor, guarded by two TPV soldiers with wind-up crossbows and a biometric lock that no longer worked. Ariga pressed his palm against a dormant plate. Nothing happened.

"The SDD sections," he said. "Comms, command, and what's left of our supply. The power came back on days ago, but this level never reboots. The breakers threw themselves and no one can reach them by hand."

Beyond the hatch, barely audible through the plating, a faint hum pulsed—then died, then pulsed again. The comms equipment was alive, but barely. Not enough to respond. Not enough to connect.

Kael stepped toward the hatch. "Can you access it from out here?"

Behind him, Ariga frowned at the half-muttered question.

Iris answered in Kael's mind. "The hatch's authentication subsystem is offline. The terminal beyond it retains a partial power state — one communication line, weak, and unresponsive to manual input."

"And the SDD?"

"The soldiers have been attempting a connection to the Regional Command in Kyoto for weeks," Iris said. "They are broadcasting across every standard band and receiving nothing. Their signal never leaves the shelter."

Kael said nothing.

"I can do it differently." Her voice was quieter now. "The terminal's communication line carries the shelter's registered signal data and its encryption keys. I can intercept that data as it cycles, and transmit through my own system — using the shelter's identity, but carried on my own connection. Kyoto will receive it as a legitimate shelter status report. The soldiers will never know it wasn't routed through their own dead equipment."

She waited.

"I will need your approval. This is not an authorized use of my systems."

Kael looked at the sleeping, dying floors above them.

"Do it," he said quietly.

He could feel her work—not through the terminal's circuits, but around them. Pulling the dormant signal data. Reading the routing tables. Building the report in the shelter's own voice, then slipping it out through her own unseen connection.

"Understood."

A single transmission, brief and clean, left the shelter minutes later.

And outside, the first wave hit.

---

The onslaught began just after dusk.

Mutated crows, hundreds of them, broke against the dome first—driven, frenzied, hurling themselves at the metal plating. Then the ground animals came. Boars. Dogs. A tide of twisted fur and muscle, surging against the two rings of High Mobility Vehicles Ariga's men had raised.

Kael fought.

Minutes bled into hours. The defenders cycled in shifts; Kael didn't. He met the second wave at the barricade line, spear carving through hide and sinew, Cherry a white storm at the flank, shredding anything that slipped past.

The SDD soldiers held the flanks with wind-up crossbows and air rifles, bolt after bolt, rationing every shaft.

The third wave came at midnight.

Kael's arms were raw. His spear had been re-shafted twice. Cherry moved slower now, blood matted thick over her white coat, but she never stopped.

On the fourth wave, near dawn, the line nearly broke.

A beast bigger than the boar—a mutated sika deer, antlers grown into a crown of blade-like bone—smashed through the outer ring of vehicles. It trampled one soldier, gored another, and drove the center of the line stumbling backward.

Kael met it head-on.

The antlers raked his shoulder. He took the hit, dropped low, and drove the spear up into the soft tissue beneath the deer's chin. The creature crashed down, kicking, and Kael pinned it until it stilled.

Seven hours.

Four waves.

The last mutated animal crumpled at the edge of the barricade, and the clearing fell silent.

---

Then the horizon opened.

A low, rising howl shook the morning air—not a Beast, but turbines. Mag-lev repulsors. A formation of shapes descending through the grey Kyoto haze at impossible speed.

The Leviathans came first.

Twenty of them. Omni-Transit "Leviathan" cruisers, their armored hulls scarred black with boarding-action paint, moving in a tight echelon like a herd of iron behemoths sweeping over the rooftops. City buses that had been repurposed into war transports—electrified plating humming, hatches open, soldiers silhouetted in every doorway.

Behind them, the heaviest of them all.

Five TPV "Juggernaut" Strike-Cruisers.

Fortresses made to hover. Massive slabs of dark overlapping armor, roof-mounted twin-plasma cannons angled forward, dropping through the dawn in a staggered wall of shadow. Localized gravity fields crushed rubble beneath their hulls as they came to rest, and the ground itself seemed to groan under their weight.

The dome's defenders stared up, weapons forgotten.

"Those are... ours," Ariga whispered.

Hatches hissed open. TPV soldiers poured out in full exoskeleton combat suits—nearly a hundred of them—broad-shouldered, heavy-plated, rifles raised as they fanned into the clearing. Behind them, SDD general units disembarked from the Leviathans, dispatching medical teams and supply crates before the repulsors had fully settled.

Ninety-four combat troops from the Juggernauts.

Forty SDD general personnel from the Leviathans.

A relief force out of the Kyoto Black Citadel, moving with the calm, mechanical precision of an institution that had manufactured war for generations.

The lead Juggernaut lowered its ramp, and a Sub-Prime in full field plate stepped out, scanning the carnage of the killing ground.

"Captain-Prime Ariga," he called across the clearing. "Kyoto received your shelter's status report. Consider this your resupply."

Ariga turned to look at Kael.

Kael said nothing. Ariga said nothing. Neither man acknowledged the transmission that had never been a shelter report at all.

The Sub-Prime continued, louder now, addressing the war-torn line:

"All displaced civilians will be evacuated to the Citadel immediately. We're securing this shelter and rolling it back into active service."

He paused, and looked at the young man with the spear standing at the center of the barricades.

"Stand down, civilian. Your work here is done."
