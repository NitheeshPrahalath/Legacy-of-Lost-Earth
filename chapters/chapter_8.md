# **Chapter 8**

**Automated Retail Store, Osaka Ruins \| September 11, 2120, 09:30**

Hirota's eyes widened as if he couldn't believe what he was seeing.
Then, all at once, his expression broke into a trembling smile.\
"Yes, Kael---it's me. Hirota Ren," he said, voice unsteady but filled
with relief. "I never thought I'd see someone I know again\... someone
still alive."

But then his tone shifted, the joy breaking into something fragile. "I
never thought... I'd see anyone again."\
His voice cracked. The words caught in his throat.\
And then---he fell to his knees, shoulders shaking.\
"I never... thought..."

Kael froze for a moment before kneeling down beside him, placing a
steadying hand on his shoulder. Hirota's sobs came raw and desperate.
Kael didn't say anything---he simply held him there.

For the first time since the world fell apart, Kael felt guilt---not for
surviving, but for *doubting* someone who once shared his world.

The others stayed silent. The woman lowered her pulse gun. The older man
eased his stance. The tension slowly drained from the room.

Hirota's voice came again, broken and trembling.\
"My father... he died protecting me and my mother. His last words were
to keep her safe---to be strong. I tried, Kael. I fought everything...
those giant mosquitoes, the beetles, even a mutated dog. We survived so
many nights."\
He wiped his eyes, shaking his head. "But I couldn't protect her. She
saved me in the end. She pushed me away and..." His voice broke again.
"Her last words were the same as his---'Be safe.'"

No one spoke. Even Cherry went still, the faint sound of her purring
fading into the silence.

The old man---gray-haired, firm but kind-eyed---walked forward. He
placed a weathered hand on Hirota's shoulder.\
"You've suffered much, young man," he said in a voice that carried quiet
strength. "But hardship forges the will to live. Remember their words.
Remember their sacrifices. They wanted you safe---so live. Grow
stronger, and survive for their sake."

His words seemed to ripple through the group. For a moment, even Kael
felt something stir---a reminder of what it meant to keep moving
forward.

Iris's voice whispered in Kael's mind.\
"Kael, that man... his posture, his tone. He's a retired **TPV
officer**. I can tell from his discipline."

Kael nodded slightly in thought. The man's presence radiated command,
calm and control---something the world rarely had anymore.

Hirota slowly stood, wiping the last of his tears. "Thank you, Tanaka...
," he said, "If not for you that day, I'd have been torn apart by that
flock of mutated crows."

He turned back toward Kael, forcing a faint smile. "This is Kael
Ardyn---he's from my college, Osaka Metaverse College. We---"

But the woman suddenly cut him off, voice sharp with suspicion.\
"I don't care who he is," she said coldly, pointing toward Kael. "I want
to know *why* he has that cat. Is it mutated like the others?"

Instantly, the air grew tense again.

The old man, Tanaka, raised a hand, steadying the moment. "Kael," he
said evenly. "I trust Hirota, and I want to trust you. But you
understand our concern. That cat---you're sure it's safe? Not mutated?"

Inside Kael's mind, Iris's voice came quick and firm.\
"Kael, no matter what---don't reveal Cherry's mutation or your increased
strength from the mutated meat. It will only draw danger."

Kael's arms tightened around Cherry as she nestled closer into his
chest. He gave a small nod, composing himself before speaking aloud.

"Actually," he said, voice calm, "her name's Cherry. I found her a few
days ago in a food distribution hub. She's the only normal animal I've
seen since the world went to hell. I... wanted to protect that little
bit of sanity left."

The woman's expression softened slightly. The tension in the room eased.
Even Tanaka seemed satisfied with the answer.

"I see," Tanaka said after a moment. "A remnant of peace in chaos...
That's worth holding onto." He crossed his arms. "Tell me then,
Kael---where have you been since the disaster struck?"

Kael looked past them, through the broken windows of the shop, where the
morning light bled through the red-stained sky.

Kael told them his story---how he had traveled from the ruins of Nara
Prefecture to the shattered remains of Osaka, surviving day after day in
a world turned hostile. He spoke plainly, leaving out any mention of
Iris. There were things they didn't need to know---at least not yet.

By the time he finished, silence filled the air.\
Even the woman who had first pointed her gun at him looked uneasy.

They had all suffered, but Kael's story was different.\
He had survived **alone**---with no family, no group, no support---just
his will and a single stray cat.

Hirota lowered his head, guilt flickering in his eyes. He'd cried over
losing his mother, and here was Kael---someone who had faced the same,
yet carried his pain without breaking.

Kael noticed their faces, the quiet pity forming in their eyes. He shook
his head and forced a small smile.\
"There's no need to feel bad for me," he said evenly. "Honestly, I think
I'm lucky compared to most people. On my way here, I saw what became of
many---people who couldn't survive, who... turned into something else."

The group fell silent again, the weight of his words pressing down.

After a moment, Tanaka stepped closer. He placed a firm, steady hand on
Kael's shoulder and looked him straight in the eye.\
"Boy," he said, his voice rough yet kind, "a strong mind is tempered on
a path of fire. You've walked through it---and come out standing. That's
what matters now."

Kael met his gaze and nodded, feeling a quiet respect for the old man.

Cherry, still in his arms, gave a soft mewl---as if agreeing.

The group finally lowered their defenses, the tension fading from the
air. For the first time since entering the store, Kael could breathe
freely.

Tanaka was the first to speak. His voice was calm, but his eyes stayed
sharp and assessing.\
"Kael," he said, "you mentioned earlier that we could help each other.
What did you mean by that?"

Before Kael could answer, Iris's voice echoed softly in his mind.\
*"Kael, it's better to be honest here. Tell them what you saw. Say you
noticed the Yodo River from afar---that the destruction didn't seem to
have crossed it."*

Kael nodded subtly and spoke, "I'm sure you've all seen it---the fire
still spreading, devouring everything in its path. If we stay here,
there won't be much left soon."

At his words, the atmosphere grew heavy. Even Cherry's faint purr went
quiet. Everyone knew the truth he spoke of---there was no safety left
here.

"But," Kael continued, a faint spark in his eyes, "I believe there's
still a chance to survive."

His words drew every gaze back to him.\
Tanaka raised an eyebrow. "You're talking about crossing the river,
aren't you?"

Kael blinked, caught off guard. "Yes. While coming from Nara, I saw the
Yodo River from afar. The other side looked... stable. The damage didn't
seem to have spread that far."

Tanaka nodded slowly, folding his arms. "Yes... because of the
river---and the **Sakai Logistics Fortress** presence on the other
side---it's possible there are survivors there. Maybe even a safe zone."

The words stunned everyone. Even Kael froze for a second.\
*A **Joint Base**?* he thought.

Tanaka continued, "But the problem isn't about what's across the
river---it's *crossing* it. The Yodo's wide, and if the **Sakai
Logistics Fortress** still active, they're likely under siege. Mutated
beasts don't care about borders. So tell me, Kael..." His sharp eyes
turned to him. "How exactly do you plan to cross that river?"

The room went quiet. Every gaze landed on Kael.

Iris's voice echoed softly in his mind.\
*"Kael, tell them about increasing our strength---about the mutated
meat."*

Kael tensed. *"Iris, are you serious? They'll think I've lost my
mind---or worse."*

*"Trust me,"* she replied calmly. *"It will work out."*

Kael exhaled deeply and looked up at the group.\
"There is a way," he said finally, his tone measured. "But it requires
strength---more than what any of us have right now."

The woman frowned. "Strength? You mean finding more people? Because if
that's the case, I'm against it. The more people we bring in, the more
danger we invite. You can never trust human nature---not anymore."

Several others nodded in agreement.

Kael shook his head. "No. I don't mean numbers. I mean increasing our
*own* strength---physically. No matter how many weapons or people we
gather, they'll all run out eventually. But if we can strengthen our
bodies, we'll have a real chance to fight and survive."

The group stared at him, confused and wary.

He continued quietly, "I've found a way to do that. You've all seen how
mutated beasts differ from normal animals---their size, speed, power.
The method I discovered... is to consume the meat of those mutated
creatures."

The silence that followed was absolute.

Even Tanaka looked momentarily stunned. His brows drew together. "You
mean to tell us,"He said slowly, "that you want us to *eat* the meat of
those things?"

Kael nodded once. "Yes. I discovered it by accident. When I was
starving, I had no choice but to eat the body of a mutated dog. I
should've died... but instead, I grew stronger."

The young girl gagged at his words, burying her face in her mother's
chest. The woman shot Kael a look of horror and disgust, while Hirota
and the other man exchanged uneasy glances.

The woman turned sharply toward Kael, disbelief and suspicion in her
eyes.\
"You've been *eating* the meat from those mutated animals?" she
demanded. "How could you even think of that? We humans have always lived
on nutrient pods and energy capsules. That's what food *is*! And you're
saying it didn't change you? You still look normal... how is that
possible? Don't tell me you've become one of those monsters!"

Kael raised both hands quickly, shaking his head. "No---no, I haven't
become any kind of monster. I ate what I could to survive. When
everything collapsed, there was no other choice. Besides..." His tone
softened. "Humans once relied on animals and plants for food---long
before synthetic nutrition existed. With proper heating and cooking, the
meat from these mutated beasts can still be eaten safely."

The group exchanged uneasy glances, clearly taken aback. This was
something none of them had ever heard before.

Tanaka stroked his beard thoughtfully, his voice calm but edged with
curiosity.\
"Yes... it's true that humanity once consumed animal and plant matter as
food. More than a fifty years ago, before the **Global Conservation
Acts**, nations agreed to replace traditional food sources with nutrient
pods and energy capsules---to prevent further harm to nature. Even
during my time, the shift was already complete. Only in my father's
generation did people still eat what they called 'natural food.'"

He looked back at Kael, his eyes narrowing slightly.\
"While what you say makes sense, there's still something I don't
understand. How did you *find out* this was possible? And how can we be
sure it's safe? What if the meat only affected *you* this way? What if
it harms the rest of us?"
