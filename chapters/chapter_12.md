# **Chapter 12**

**Yodo River Bridge / Connecting Landmass \| September 11, 2120, 14:30**

The surge of adrenaline drained away, leaving behind a cold, biting
pain.

Kael tried to lower his spear, but his left arm refused to obey. A
searing burn tore through his shoulder, as if molten metal had fused
into the joint. He glanced down---his sleeve was shredded, blood
spreading in thick, dark streaks.

"Kael, your heart rate is spiking. Blood loss is ongoing," Iris said,
her tone sharp and precise. "Lacerations are approximately three
centimeters deep. Immediate compression required."

Kael didn't answer. His attention had already shifted.

Nakamura was beside Tanaka, gripping him tightly.

"Father! Stay with me---look at me!"

Tanaka's face had gone pale, almost grey. His breathing was shallow,
uneven. When he tried to speak, a thin line of blood slipped from the
corner of his mouth.

"I'm... fine..." he whispered, unfocused. "Just... a moment..."

Maya moved in quickly, activating her compact medical kit. The device
unfolded with a soft \*click\*, projecting a faint blue diagnostic grid
over her hands.

"Kael---sit down. Now."

He obeyed.

She scanned his shoulder; the grid flickered, mapping the depth of the
wound in real time.

"Hold still."

A sterilizing mist sprayed from the device, coating the laceration. Kael
hissed as the solution seeped in.

"Ah---!"

"Pain means it's working," Maya replied curtly.

She pressed a synthetic compression patch over the wound. The material
adhered instantly, tightening as it detected blood flow, sealing the
edges with a faint thermal pulse.

"Pressure stabilized," the kit chimed softly.

"Keep your arm still," Maya said, guiding him. "The patch will regulate
bleeding, but strain will reopen it."

Without wasting a second, she turned to Tanaka.

"Lift him---upright, slowly."

Nakamura and Hirota adjusted their hold, keeping Tanaka seated. His
breathing hitched, uneven.

Maya activated a deeper scan. The grid swept across his torso,
highlighting stress points.

"...Internal trauma likely," she muttered.

She wiped the blood from his mouth, then retrieved a small respiratory
aid---a thin, curved device---and placed it near his nose. It released a
controlled oxygen flow.

"Breathe with it. Slow. Don't force it."

Tanaka nodded weakly.

Next, she applied a flexible support band across his chest. The material
tightened automatically, stabilizing his ribcage without restricting
airflow.

"Chest stabilized," the kit confirmed.

"Do not lie him down," Maya instructed firmly. "Keep him upright.
Monitor his breathing rhythm."

Nakamura nodded, gripping his father more carefully now.

Maya exhaled, closing the kit with a soft snap.

"This is only first aid," she said, her voice steady but serious. "But
it will keep both of you from getting worse---for now."

Everyone looked at Maya with clear shock.

No one expected the usually guarded and sharp-tongued woman to handle a
critical situation with such calm precision.

Feeling their eyes on her, Maya let out a quiet sigh.

"You don't have to stare like that," she said flatly. "I know a thing or
two about emergency situations."

Nakamura bent slightly and spoke with sincerity,

"Thank you... for helping us."

He had questions---many---but chose to hold them back.

Even after her explanation, the doubt didn't fully leave their minds.

Kael spoke mentally, \*"Iris... do you think she's telling the truth?
Her behavior earlier and now---it feels contradictory."\*

Iris responded calmly,

"Kael, her earlier actions were precautionary. Her current actions
reflect her actual capability. There is no contradiction---only context.
You may lower your guard slightly. She is an asset."

Kael didn't reply, but he understood.

\-\--

After stabilizing themselves, the group carefully crossed the bridge.

Nakamura carried Tanaka on his back, steady and firm. Hirota supported
Kael from the side. Maya led the front, alert and scanning. Emily walked
quietly with Cherry in her arms.

On the other side lay a small landmass---strangely quiet.

No movement. No threats.

They decided to rest there temporarily.

Tanaka's breathing had improved, though still strained. Kael endured his
pain, slowly adjusting to it.

Meanwhile, Nakamura and Hirota dragged the corpses of the two foxes
across.

By the time the sun edged toward mid-afternoon, hunger began to creep in
again.

Kael exhaled slowly. He wasn't in any condition to prepare food.

"Iris..." he murmured mentally.

With her guidance, Kael spoke aloud,

"Nakamura, Hirota---you'll have to handle the preparation. I'll guide
you."

Under his instructions, they worked through the process. It took nearly
forty minutes, but eventually, the roasted meat was ready.

They consumed the larger fox. The smaller one was discarded.

After eating, the group rested for another hour.

Strength returned. Fatigue faded.

\-\--

"Iris, run a scan."

A moment later, the report appeared in his mind:

**Kael Ardyn -- IRIS Status Report**\
**Age:** 21\
**Time Post-Awakening:** 1 Month\
**Location:** Nakodashima, Osaka Ruins\
**Date**: September 11, 2120 \| Time: 14:45

  ------------------------------ ----------------------------------------
  **Core Attribute**             **Value**

  Strength                       **2.57**(from 1.80)

  Speed                          **2.06** (from 1.56)

  Life Force                     **2.63**(from 1.90)

  Stamina                        **2.63** (from 1.90)

  Constitution                   **2.27** (from 1.57)

  Reaction                       **0.23s** (0.26s)
  ------------------------------ ----------------------------------------

Kael blinked.

"That much...?"

\"Yes, it has been a while since we last scanned, so yes you have made
huge progress.\"

"Iris, scan Cherry too."

Moments later---

**Cherry -- IRIS Status Report**\
**Age:** 2 Months\
**Time Post-Awakening:** 1 Month\
**Location:** Nakodashima, Osaka Ruins\
**Date**: September 11, 2120 \| Time: 14:45

  -------------------------------- --------------------------------------
  **Core Attribute**               **Value**

  Strength                         **3.4**(from 2.04)

  Speed                            **9.45** (from 8.59)

  Life Force                       **3.7**(from 1.80)

  Stamina                          **3.7** (from 1.80)

  Constitution                     **3.90** (from 1.55)
  -------------------------------- --------------------------------------

Kael stared.

Then mentally exploded---

\*"Why?! How is this even fair?! She just eats and sleeps! I'm fighting
for my life out here!"\*

Iris replied, almost patiently,

"Cherry is a young, evolving organism. Growth efficiency is
significantly higher."

Kael sighed internally.

\*"Unbelievable..."\*

After a pause, he asked,

"Can you scan the others like this?"

"No. Accurate analysis requires blood samples."

"...Then estimate."

A brief scan later---

"Nakamura: 1.6

Tanaka: 1.3

Hirota: 1.2

Maya: 1.4

Emily: 1.1"

Kael processed the numbers.

"Is this enough... to cross safely?"

Iris answered without hesitation.

"I don't have enough data to be certain," Iris said.

"But if the mutated animals ahead are similar to the fox, you should be
able to make it."

"Let us hope so."

After an hour of rest, both Kael and Tanaka had stabilized
significantly. The effects of the mutated meat were undeniable.

Kael rotated his shoulder slightly. The pain was still there---but dull
now, manageable.

Tanaka, supported by Nakamura, stood on his own feet again.

"I can move," he said, taking a slow breath. "Not at full strength...
but enough."

Maya gave him a brief look. "Don't overestimate yourself."

"I won't," Tanaka replied with a faint smile.

The group resumed their journey.

They moved carefully, formation intact. Time stretched as they
advanced---step by step, scanning every shadow, every sound.

After nearly ninety minutes, they finally reached the river.

And stopped.

Silence fell over the group.

Every bridge in sight... was destroyed.

Collapsed steel, broken concrete, twisted frameworks sinking into the
flowing water.

Hirota's voice broke first.\
"...You've got to be kidding me."

Maya frowned, scanning the distance. "Every single one...?"

Nakamura's jaw tightened. "This wasn't random. These were destroyed
deliberately."

Tanaka narrowed his eyes at the ruins.\
"The **TPV** did this. They must have destroyed the bridges to stop
mutated creatures---and infected people---from crossing."

Kael continued, his tone low,\
"But they didn't realize... this mutation doesn't spread through
contact. It's something else."

He stepped forward.

The river below wasn't calm.

Dark shapes moved beneath the surface---fast, erratic.

Then---

A violent splash.

Something large surfaced for a brief moment before vanishing again.

Hirota instinctively stepped back. "...Those are fish?"

"Mutated," Maya said grimly.

The weight of the situation pressed down on them.

No bridge.\
No safe path.\
No clear solution.

Kael closed his eyes briefly.

"Iris..."

"I am already analyzing," she replied.

A pause.

Then---

"Kael, there is a possible solution."

His eyes opened. "Say it."

"These bridges are **Self-Anchored Suspension Bridges**, although
collapsed, retain partial structural integrity---specifically the
support cables and segmented platforms."

Kael looked again, more carefully this time.

Some sections... were still connected.

Unstable---but not completely gone.

"You're suggesting we cross that?"

"Yes."

Kael turned to the group. "I have a crude... and dangerous idea."

All eyes shifted to him.

"Look closely," he continued. "The bridges were destroyed in a hurry.
Some of the main support cables are still intact. If we utilize the
cables -"

Maya immediately caught on.\
"You're not seriously suggesting we cross using those cables... right?"

Kael gave a bitter smile. "It's insane. But it's the only way I see."

Nakamura thought for a moment.\
"It's possible---but risky. Not all cables are stable. One wrong
choice... and it snaps."

Tanaka studied the structure carefully.\
"There are four cables still connected. But only two are viable---the
far left and the inner right. The others are either overstretched or
have no support beneath them. If they snap... there's nothing to catch
you."

The group exchanged glances.

Then silently split.

Tanaka, Nakamura, and Hirota took the far-left cable.

Kael, Maya, Emily, and Cherry took the inner-right.

Both cables stretched over 900 meters.

A terrifying distance.

Nakamura went first.

He hung suspended beneath the steel, limbs hooked over the top of the
cable in a death-grip, dragging his body upward along the rising arc
with a slow, mechanical rhythm.

"Follow my lead," he called.

Hirota hesitated for a moment before gripping the cable tightly.

"...This is insane."

"Don't think. Just move," Nakamura replied.

Tanaka followed last, steady despite his condition.

On Kael's side---

"Iris... guide me."

\"Position yourself beneath the cable. Hook your ankles over the steel
and cross them to lock your lower body. Wrap your arms over the top,
hugging the diameter against your chest to utilize friction.

Move in three distinct phases:

-   The Reach: Release your arm grip and slide your hands forward 30
    centimeters. Re-clamp.

-   The Draw: Contract your core and lats to pull your torso toward your
    hands.

-   The Reset: Slide your locked ankles forward to meet your center of
    gravity.

Kael nodded and moved first.

Emily stood frozen behind him, staring down at the river below.

"...I-I can't..."

Maya immediately stepped closer.\
"Emily, look at me. Not down. Only at me."

Emily's breathing was shaky. "What if I fall...?"

"You won't," Maya said firmly. "I'm right behind you."

After several tense minutes, Emily finally climbed onto the cable.

Maya followed last.

Cherry perched herself on Kael's shoulder, letting out soft, anxious
meows.

They moved with agonizing slowness.

The physics of the bridge were now working against them. Having passed
the lowest point of the cable's sag, they were no longer shimmying
downward; they were **climbing**. The steel line sloped upward at an
increasingly punishing angle toward the next concrete pillar. Every
meter gained meant fighting gravity more directly, and the weight of
their own bodies felt as though it had doubled.

Pain built up steadily---a deep, throbbing ache in the lats and a
burning \"flash pump\" in the forearms.

After crossing 150 meters, the slope steepened. The cable, slick with
river mist and rust, offered less friction.

Emily's grip began to falter.

"M-Mom... my hands... they hurt..." her voice trembled. "I can't hold
on..."

Maya's expression tightened as she hugged the cable from below, her
knuckles white. "Don't let go. Just a little more, Emily. Keep moving."

"I can't... I can't feel my fingers..."

Kael glanced back. The perspective was terrifying; they were suspended
hundreds of feet above the churning water, dangling from a wire that now
felt like a vertical wall.

The strain was creeping into his own arms, a cold numbness spreading
from his wrists to his elbows.

"Iris," he said mentally, "she won't last. The incline is too steep. We
still have at least a hundred meters of climbing to reach the pillar's
ledge."

"There is no optimal solution," Iris replied, her voice cold and
analytical. "The gravitational resistance has increased by 15% due to
the current slope angle. However, you may carry her. Position her in
front of you and support her weight against your chest. It will drain
your ATP reserves exponentially. Your probability of reaching the pillar
will drop below 20%."

"It doesn't matter as long as there\'s a chance."

Behind him, Emily's legs started slipping from their cross-lock.

"Mom... I'm slipping---!"

Kael exhaled slowly, his lungs burning. He was already at his limit, and
the steepest part of the arc lay ahead.

"...No choice."

He shifted his position carefully, locking his legs tighter around the
steel to free his hands for a split second.

"Emily," he said firmly, his voice echoing over the rush of the water.
"Listen to me. I'm going to pull you onto me. Don't panic."

Her voice was a broken whisper. "...O-okay..."

"Trust me."

The crossing had just become far more dangerous. He wasn\'t just
fighting the distance anymore; he was fighting a vertical ascent with a
second life strapped to his chest.
