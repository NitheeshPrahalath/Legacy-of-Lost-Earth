# **Chapter 13**

**Yodo River Bridge Cables \| September 11, 2120, 17:30**

Kael tightened his grip and shifted his weight forward.

"On three," he said, forcing his voice to stay steady. "One...
two---move!"

Emily let go with one hand, trembling.

For a split second---

She slipped.

"MOM---!"

Her body dropped an inch before Kael lunged forward, grabbing her wrist
mid-fall.

"Don't let go!" Maya shouted from behind, her voice sharp with panic.

Kael's muscles screamed as he held her weight with one arm, his other
clamped desperately around the cable.

"I've got you!" he gritted out. "I've got you---just hold still!"

Emily was crying now, her fingers barely responding.

"I can't... I can't feel my hands..."

"Then don't use them," Kael snapped. "Use me."

With a sharp inhale, he pulled her upward, inch by inch. Every movement
felt like his shoulder was tearing apart again. The bandaged wound
burned violently, threatening to reopen.

"Iris... I'm losing strength..."

"Heart rate critical. Muscle fatigue approaching threshold," Iris
responded.\
"Reduce motion inefficiency. Lock her weight against your core."

Kael adjusted immediately, dragging Emily closer and pressing her
against his chest.

"Wrap your arms around my neck," he ordered.

"I---I can't---"

"You can. Do it!"

With a broken sob, Emily forced her arms to move, loosely clinging to
him.

Kael secured her by crossing one arm over her back, effectively pinning
her to his body.

Now---

Every movement doubled in difficulty.

He resumed climbing.

Slow. Heavy. Painful.

Each shift upward sent tremors through his arms.

Behind him, Maya's breathing had grown uneven.

"Kael... how far...?"

"Not far," he replied, though he knew it wasn't entirely true.

On the other cable---

Hirota's grip slipped slightly.

"Shit---!"

His body swung dangerously, legs losing contact for a moment.

"Nakamura!" he shouted.

"Stabilize yourself!" Nakamura barked back. "Use your legs! Don't rely
only on your arms!"

"I'm trying!"

Tanaka's strained voice followed,\
"Hirota... breathe... don't rush..."

Hirota clenched his teeth, forcing his legs back around the cable.

"...I'm okay... I'm okay..."

But his arms trembled uncontrollably.

The cable groaned under the shifting weight.

Kael's fingers tightened instinctively, knuckles whitening as the thin
metal strands bit into his skin. Sweat ran down his temples, stinging
his eyes, but he didn't dare blink for too long.

Emily's grip around his neck slackened for a terrifying moment.

"Emily," he said sharply, his voice cutting through the wind, "tighten
your arms."

"I---I'm trying..." Her voice was faint, uneven, like it was slipping
away with her strength.

"You don't try," Kael snapped, harsher than he intended. "You do it."

A weak squeeze followed. Not strong---but enough.

Behind them, Maya's voice came, strained but controlled. "Emily, stay
with us. Talk to me. Say something."

"I... I don't know what to say..."

"Anything. Don't go quiet."

Kael shifted his right hand forward along the cable, inching ahead. His
left arm screamed in protest as it kept Emily anchored against him. His
shoulder throbbed violently, each pulse sharper than the last.

The world narrowed to three things---

Grip.

Breath.

Movement.

Below, the water surged again.

A loud splash echoed upward.

Kael ignored it.

"Iris," he muttered through clenched teeth, "status."

"Muscle output decreasing. Estimated functional strength at sixty-one
percent and falling."

"...That fast...?"

"Additional load exceeding optimal threshold."

"No kidding..."

His hand slipped half an inch.

Kael froze.

Every muscle locked instantly as his body swung slightly with the
cable's movement. Emily let out a small cry, tightening instinctively.

"Don't react," Iris said immediately. "Stabilize first."

Kael inhaled slowly through his nose.

The cable swayed... then steadied.

He exhaled.

"Okay..." he whispered. "Okay."

He moved again.

One hand forward.

Legs tighten.

Pull.

The rhythm was breaking. His body no longer followed clean
sequences---each movement now required conscious effort, like dragging
himself through thick mud.

Behind him, Maya faltered.

"Ah---!"

The sudden sound snapped his focus back.

"Maya?" Kael called without turning.

"I'm fine---!" she replied quickly, but her breathing betrayed her. It
was ragged now, uneven. "Just... keep going."

"You slipped."

"Just for a second."

Kael's jaw tightened.

"Don't lie."

"...I'm still moving, aren't I?" she shot back, a hint of frustration
cutting through her exhaustion.

That was answer enough.

Ahead, the pillar loomed closer---its jagged steel beams twisted and
blackened, remnants of whatever force had torn the bridge apart.

It still looked impossibly far.

"Twenty-five meters," Iris stated.

Kael let out a shaky breath, his fingers tightening around the cable as
his arms trembled.

"Feels like two hundred..."

Emily shifted weakly against him, her grip barely holding.

"Kael... my arms..."

"They're still there," he said, forcing steadiness into his voice.
"That's all that matters."

"I can't feel them..."

Kael adjusted his hold, one arm locking tighter around the cable as he
carefully supported her.

"Then let me feel them for you."

He pulled one of her hands into his, pressing it firmly.

"Don't let go. Not even a little."

Every second stretched longer than the last. The strain didn't plateau.
It kept rising, digging deeper into muscle and bone. His shoulders
burned. His fingers felt like they were being peeled open.

Iris' voice cut in.

"Muscle fatigue approaching critical threshold. Grip strength
declining."

"I know," Kael muttered under his breath.

They kept moving.

Slow.

Ten minutes felt like an hour.

Finally, the pillar came into reach.

"Almost there," Kael said, his voice rough. "Emily... stay with me."

No response.

He pushed the last stretch purely on instinct.

Reaching the pillar, Kael shifted first, forcing his numb fingers to
release one at a time. He lifted Emily up and placed her onto the
concrete platform.

"Sit. Don't move."

He pulled himself up next, then immediately turned and extended his
hand.

"Maya."

Maya was breathing hard, her face pale, but she grabbed his arm without
hesitation. He hauled her up.

On the other cable, similar movements played out.

Nakamura climbed up first, then turned back sharply.

"Hirota, move!"

"I---I'm trying---"

"Don't think. Move!"

Tanaka followed slower, but steady. Nakamura anchored himself and helped
both of them onto the pillar.

For a moment, no one spoke.

They were drenched. Sweat soaked through everything. Hands trembling.
Breathing uneven.

Cherry landed lightly beside Kael, completely stable, tail flicking. Its
eyes scanned the surroundings.

Maya broke the silence first, her voice low.

"Eat. Now."

Kael nodded. No argument.

They pulled out the roasted mutated meat from their bags. The smell
alone was enough to stir something primal. It was far better than the
bland nutrient pods or synthetic meals they'd relied on before.

"Eat," Kael said. "Your body needs it."

They did.

The meat went down fast.

Warmth spread through their bodies.

Iris updated.

"Kael, your body energy is being restored, but the energy consumed was
very high. Your energy will only be restored by 60% after the
consumption."

Kael exhaled slowly.

"Not enough..."

Across the gap, Nakamura's group wasn't faring any better.

Hirota sat slumped, arms hanging limp.

"I can't feel my hands..."

Tanaka gave a faint, tired smile.

Nakamura glanced over at Kael.

"We push?"

Kael shook his head slightly.

"Few more minutes."

Nakamura nodded once.

The distance between the pillars was only a few meters. Close enough to
talk. Close enough to see the strain on each other's faces.

After a short rest, Kael stood first.

"Let us move now."

They returned to the cables.

The next section sloped downward.

Iris spoke directly to Kael, "Excessive oscillation may destabilize both
lines."

"Careful," Kael warned. "Too much force and the cable will swing."

They moved slower this time.

The descent was easier on their arms---but harder on balance.

Any sudden movement caused the cable to sway.

Emily clenched her teeth.

Fifteen minutes later, they reached the lower end.

Kael dropped down first, knees bending slightly on impact. He turned
immediately, watching the others land one by one.

It took them nearly forty minutes to cross the first half.

The second arc began shortly after.

Shorter. but their bodies were already worn down.

"Same pace," Nakamura called from the other side.

They moved again.

This time, Emily crossed on her own.

Slow. Careful. Shaking---but she didn't stop.

Kael stayed just close enough.

Twenty-five minutes later, they reached the second pillar.

No one wasted time.

The group ate roasted mutated meat in smaller quantity.

Ten minutes.

That's all they gave themselves.

Silence hung heavier now. Each and every one of them were exhausted.

"Kael, it is 18:30. Nocturnal mutated animals will become active soon."
Iris noted.

The sun had already gone down. The night was slowly creeping in and
night was more dangerous, night was the time when most animals were
active, the same applied to the mutated animals.

They have to cross the bridge soon.

Then they descended the second arc.

Faster this time. About ten minutes.

And then---

The third arc.

Kael looked up.

Same length as the first.

The group's faces went pale.

Every one of them was exhausted now. Arms trembling. Grip weakening.
Breathing uneven.

Iris spoke, tone unchanged.

"Current condition: severely fatigued. Probability of failure
increased."

Kael tightened his grip on the cable, forcing strength back into his
fingers.

"Everyone... this is the last hurdle," he said, voice strained but firm.
"Once we climb this, we reach the other side."

He looked ahead, then back at them.

"Be confident. Just this one more."

Tanaka let out a slow breath, then spoke, his voice calm despite the
situation.

"Where there is a will, there is a way."

He lifted his head slightly.

"We've crossed this far. Do you really think we should fall now?"

A faint, tired smile formed.

"Believe in success... and success will find us."

No one replied.

But they held on tighter.

They started climbing.

Slow. Steady. Every movement deliberate.

Kael secured Emily against him again, just like the first arc. One arm
for the cable. One for her.

Cherry remained perched on his shoulders, body low, eyes alert.

They had barely moved around eighty meters from the pillar---

Creak.

A sharp, unnatural sound cut through the air.

Then another.

Everyone froze.

"What was that...?" Maya whispered.

They turned.

And what they saw drained whatever color remained in their faces.

A group of mutated crows.

Each one massive---at least three times the size of a normal crow. Their
black feathers reflected the dim light like blades. Their beaks... wet.
Dark red. Fresh blood still clinging.

Against the backdrop of distant firestorms and a dying sky, they looked
less like animals---

and more like a legion of death.

Emily's voice trembled.

"Kael..."

Kael's jaw tightened.

"Climb. Fast."

His voice cut through the air.

"We don't have time to waste!"

"Hurry!" Nakamura shouted from the other cable.

Fear hit all of them at once.

Raw. Immediate.

They were suspended in midair---with nowhere to run.

For a brief moment, their bodies responded.

Adrenaline surged.

Muscles pushed past limits.

They climbed faster.

Faster than before.

Faster than they should have been able to.

But it didn't last.

It never did.

Even with that burst, they only made it another forty-six meters---

before the crows reached the cables.

Kael's grip tightened.

"...damn it."

They had no free hands.

No stable footing.

No energy left.

They were... exposed.

The cable swayed faintly beneath them. Wind cut across bare hands. Forty
meters of empty air below.

Perfect prey.

"Don't move," Maya whispered. "Maybe... maybe they won't notice---"

Even as she said it, she knew.

It was useless.

The crows were already locking onto them.

Seven... eight of them.

Closing in.

Hirota's voice broke.

"...so this is it..."

Tears streamed down his face.

"I couldn't even... fulfill my parents' last wish... I---"

Emily began to cry silently, her body shaking against Kael.

"I don't want to die..."

No one had an answer.

Even Kael---

went silent.

They had strength to fight.

They had killed before.

But not like this.

Not now.

Their bodies were already at their limit just holding on.

Iris spoke.

Cold. Precise. Unwavering.

"Kael. If you release your grip and fall, probability of survival
increases."

"You may sustain severe injury. However, it provides a viable chance to
escape the swarm."

"The others will act as distraction."

Silence.

Kael didn't respond.

He didn't need to.

Iris understood.

She stopped.

Because she already knew his answer.

Kael's grip only tightened.

The first crow lunged.

Straight at Maya.

Its shrill cry pierced the air as its massive claws extended---black,
sharp, stained with dried blood.

Maya pressed herself against the cable, eyes wide.

"---!"

Then---

A flash.

Something blurred between her and the crow.

Three clean cuts.

The crow's body split mid-motion.

It dropped.

Thud.

Dead.

For a fraction of a second, everything froze.

"What...?" Nakamura breathed.

The other crows didn't hesitate.

No fear. No awareness.

They continued their attack.

Then---

Flash.

Flash.

Flash.

Each movement too fast to follow.

Each strike precise.

Each crow---

cut down.

Bodies fell one after another, hitting the ground far below.

Thud.

Thud

Thud.c

The entire exchange---

lasted only seconds.

From the first attack to the last corpse hitting the ground---

no more than seven seconds.

Silence followed.

Heavy. Unreal.

Kael\'s group didn\'t move.

Couldn\'t.

Maya\'s knuckles were white against the cable. Emily had stopped crying
--- not from relief, but from something she couldn\'t name yet.

Below them, the crows lay broken and still.
