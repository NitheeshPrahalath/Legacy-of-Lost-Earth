# Chapter 17

**Underground wood Chamber \| September 15, 2120, 9:00**

\"Mom\... Dad\...\"

The scent from the fruit stirred memories Kael had buried deep within
himself.

His mother\'s gentle smile.

His father\'s reassuring hand on his shoulder.

Tears slowly welled in his eyes.

\"Kael.\"

Iris spoke softly.

\"Your emotional state has become unstable.\"

Kael lowered his head.

\"\...I was just remembering them. My parents..\"

His voice trembled slightly.

\"But knowing I\'ll never see them again\...\"

He couldn\'t finish the sentence.

Then Iris spoke.

\"I will remain by your side.\"

\"As long as I continue functioning\...\"

\"You will not face this world alone.\"

Kael closed his eyes for a moment.

\"\...Thank you, Iris.\"

\"Interesting.\"

A third voice suddenly echoed through the chamber.

This time---

Both Kael and Iris heard it.

\"I initially believed the device on your wrist was merely another
artificial intelligence.\"

A brief pause followed.

\"But it possesses independent reasoning\...\"

\"And something resembling genuine emotion.\"

Iris remained silent for nearly two seconds. On Kael\'s wrist, the
device\'s metallic-blue interface flickered, a rapid, erratic pulse
before settling.

\"Kael, is this the voice you mentioned?\"

\"Yes.\"

The calm voice answered.

\"I am the one speaking.\"

\"You were also correct.\"

\"I brought this human here.\"

\"There is a purpose.\"

A moment later, the small tree before Kael gently swayed.

\"Eat the fruit. Completely. Even the core.\"

\"Do not overthink it.\"

Kael immediately stepped forward.

\"Who are you? Why did you bring me here? And where is Cherry?\"

The voice answered without the slightest hesitation.

\"It is not yet time for you to know who I am.\"

\"As for your remaining questions\... The fruit will help you in the
future.\"

\"You need not understand why.\"

\"You need only eat it.\"

Kael\'s expression darkened.

\"And Cherry?\"

A brief silence followed.

\"If you choose not to trust me\...\"

\"You may never leave this place.\"

\"\...Nor will you see your little Cherry again.\"

\"You have fifteen minutes.\"

The gentle voice never became louder.

Yet the final sentence carried an indescribable coldness.

The voice faded.

\"Wait!\"

Kael shouted.

\"Wait!\"

\"At least answer me!\"

The heavy, damp air of the underground chamber seemed to swallow his
voice whole.

Only silence remained.

Nothing replied.

Iris finally broke the silence.

\"Kael.\"

\"I believe the voice has ended.\"

\"Continuing to call out is unlikely to produce a response.\"

Kael slowly lowered his head.

\"Iris\...\"

He looked at the metallic-blue fruit.

\"What do we do?\"

He clenched his fists.

\"We can\'t trust that\... thing.\"

\"Until now, every mutated creature we\'ve encountered has acted on
instinct.\"

\"This one\... It speaks\.... It thinks.\"

\"It planned all of this.\"

His gaze remained fixed on the fruit.

\"But if I refuse\...\"

His voice became quieter.

\"\...Cherry.\"

A long silence filled the chamber.

\"It only gave us fifteen minutes.\"

\"If I make the wrong decision\...\"

\"I\'m afraid neither of us will ever leave this place alive.\"

Iris silently analyzed Kael\'s condition.

His fear. His hesitation.

His overwhelming desire to save Cherry immediately.

And the part of him that feared eating the fruit would bind him to the
giant tree or transform him into something that could one day threaten
the people he wanted to protect.

After processing every available possibility, Iris finally spoke.

\"Kael.\"

\"My recommendation is to consume the fruit.\"

Kael looked toward the watch.

\"\...You\'re serious?\"

\"Yes.\"

Her tone remained calm and unwavering.

\"Based on all available evidence, the giant tree has displayed no
intention of harming us.\"

\"It possessed countless opportunities to kill every member of our
group, yet it chose not to.\"

\"It released Emily unharmed.\"

\"It brought you here alive.\"

\"And its sole request has remained unchanged.\"

\"Consume the fruit.\"

She paused briefly before continuing.

\"Our current situation offers no viable alternative.\"

\"This chamber has no visible exit.\"

\"Our food supply is nonexistent.\"

\"If the giant tree abandons us here, survival becomes statistically
improbable regardless of whether we eat the fruit.\"

Kael remained silent.

\"Therefore\...\"

\"The option with the highest probability of survival is to comply.\"

Another brief pause followed.

\"And\...\"

\"For Cherry\'s sake\...\"

\"I believe this decision carries the greatest chance of seeing Cherry
again.\"

The chamber fell silent once more.

Kael slowly looked back at the strange fruit growing from the small
tree.

\"\...Alright.\"

He slowly reached toward it.

It was the first real fruit he had ever seen.

Before the catastrophe, natural fruits had become government-controlled
resources. Private cultivation had long been prohibited, and
fruit-bearing trees had disappeared from cities, replaced by ornamental
trees that bore no fruit.

Yet now\...

A single fruit rested in front of him.

Its smooth metallic-blue skin reflected the pale green light surrounding
the chamber.

Thin crimson veins pulsed rhythmically beneath its surface.

It almost looked\...

Alive.

Kael slowly wrapped his fingers around the fruit.

The instant he plucked it from the branch---

The tiny tree trembled.

Its vibrant green leaves rapidly faded into gray.

The bark cracked.

Within seconds, the entire tree had withered into a lifeless husk.

Kael instinctively stepped backward.

The decay didn\'t stop.

It spread into the wooden floor.

Across the walls.

Then the ceiling.

Every glowing branch surrounding the chamber rapidly dried and crumbled
away.

The enclosed nest disappeared before his eyes.

A scorching wave of heat immediately rushed in from every direction.

The temperature climbed at an alarming rate.

\"\...Kael.\"

For the first time since they had met, Iris hesitated before speaking.

\"\...My previous calculation was incorrect.\"

Kael immediately turned toward his wrist.

\"What happened?\"

\"The surrounding temperature has exceeded my predicted model.\"

A brief pause followed.

\"I underestimated our depth.\"

Her voice remained composed, but there was an unfamiliar heaviness
behind it.

\"Correction.\"

\"We are not several hundred meters beneath the surface.\"

\"\...We are more than fourteen kilometers underground.\"

Kael\'s eyes widened.

\"What?\"

\"The ambient temperature has already exceeded one hundred and fifty
degrees Celsius.\"

\"It is continuing to rise.\"

Another brief pause.

\"\...Kael.\"

\"Your body will not survive prolonged exposure.\"

Kael instinctively looked around.

Without the wooden chamber protecting him, all that remained was a
massive underground cavern glowing with hint of crimson rock.

Heat distorted the air itself.

\"What do we do?\"

His voice carried genuine urgency.

\"How do we get out of here?\"

Iris remained silent for a moment.

When she finally answered\...

It wasn\'t with certainty.

\"\...I no longer possess a better solution.\"

That single sentence made Kael pause.

\"The highest probability of survival\...\"

\"\...is to trust that the giant tree intends to keep its promise.\"

Another brief silence.

\"\...Please.\"

\"Finish the fruit.\"

Kael looked at the strange fruit in his hand.

Then at the blazing cavern surrounding him.

Without another word---

He shoved the entire fruit into his mouth.

Three hurried gulps.

He didn\'t even chew.

Fear left no room to care about its taste.

The fruit disappeared into his stomach almost instantly.

Kael immediately looked upward.

\"I\'ve eaten it!\"

His voice echoed throughout the cavern.

\"I\'ve done what you asked!\"

\"Now send me back!\"

Only silence answered.

Seconds passed.

The heat continued climbing.

\"Kael.\"

Iris spoke again.

\"Your internal body temperature is rising rapidly.\"

But Kael barely heard her.

\"Hey!\"

\"I finished it!\"

\"So send me---\"

His vision suddenly blurred.

The cavern spun violently.

His knees buckled beneath him.

Darkness swallowed his consciousness.

His body collapsed onto the scorching ground.

\"Kael.\"

No response.

\"Kael.\"

Her voice became louder.

\"Wake up.\"

She immediately discharged a controlled electrical impulse through his
nervous system.

No response.

A second pulse.

Nothing.

A third.

Still nothing.

His nervous system had shut itself down to protect the brain from
catastrophic overload.

Meanwhile, the temperature continued rising.

One hundred eighty degrees Celsius.

Two hundred and forty degrees Celsius .

The surrounding rocks started to glow a hint of crimson.

Iris continuously monitored the remaining time.

Ten seconds.

Nine.

Eight.

Seven.

She waited.

Surely\...

The giant tree would return.

Six.

Five.

Four.

Three.

Two.

One.

Zero.

Silence.

Nothing happened.

The countdown continued.

Plus one second.

Plus ten.

Plus thirty.

One minute.

Still nothing.

Kael\'s body temperature continued climbing.

Two hundred and eighty degrees.

Three hundred degrees Celsius.

Smoke slowly began rising from his clothes.

His skin glowed a deep crimson beneath the unbearable heat.

Yet\...

His heartbeat continued.

Even Iris\'s Quantum-Confined Boron Nitride Graphene housing had begun
absorbing the overwhelming heat.

The cavern itself had become a furnace.

Three hundred and fifty degrees Celsius.

Four hundred.

The stone beneath Kael slowly turned red-hot.

\"\...My recommendation\...\"

Iris quietly whispered.

A long pause followed.

\"\...was incorrect.\"

For the first time\...

She questioned her own decision.

Had she condemned Kael by telling him to trust the tree?

Had there been another path she had failed to calculate?

The temperature surged again.

Four hundred and fifty degrees Celsius.

Flames erupted throughout the cavern because of the dried wood.

The rocks glowed like molten metal.

Everything became an ocean of crimson fire.

\"\...I\'m sorry, Kael.\"

Just then---

A voice drifted through the burning cavern.

\"My apologies\...\"

\"I kept you waiting.\"

It wasn\'t loud.

It didn\'t echo.

Yet every word reached the deepest corners of the cavern with perfect
clarity, as though the world itself had fallen silent just to listen.

The voice was impossibly gentle.

Soft enough to calm a frightened child.

Warm enough to melt away despair.

It carried the tenderness of a mother embracing her child after a long
journey home.

Yet beneath that warmth\...

Lay an indescribable authority.

Not the authority of a king.

Nor the pressure of an overwhelming predator.

It was the quiet authority of existence itself.

An ancient presence before which even the raging inferno seemed
insignificant.

Then---

The world stopped.

The dancing flames froze in midair.

The surging heat became perfectly still.

Rising smoke halted where it drifted.

Molten rock ceased to flow.

Even the violent currents of scorching air stood motionless.

Time itself seemed to have forgotten how to move.

Silence.

Absolute.

Complete.

Only that gentle presence remained.

As though the entire underground world had paused\... simply because it
had arrived.
