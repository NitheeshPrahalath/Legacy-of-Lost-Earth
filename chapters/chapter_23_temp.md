
# Chapter 23

**Kyoto Shelter Complex, Mountain Foothills | October 15, 2120 | 15:40**

The dome's inner door groaned open.

A dim, amber-lit corridor stretched downward, descending into the earth at a sharp angle. The air was stale—warm, recycled, and heavy with the smell of bodies and old air filtration.

"Fifty-two meters," Iris said quietly. "Three floors."

Kael stepped inside alone, Cherry at his heels. Ariga led him down, the other fourteen soldiers fanning out to hold the entrance.

The first floor hit him like a wall.

Hundreds of civilians lay packed together across the reinforced floor, shoulder to shoulder in narrow rows. Makeshift bedding. Strips of torn uniform serving as bandages. Every few meters, someone was crying.

And every few meters, someone was dying.

An elderly man convulsed violently against the floor, body arching, teeth grinding, veins dark against his skin. A woman beside him held his head, screaming for help no doctor came to give. Further down the row, a young boy went quiet mid-cry—then began shaking, eyes rolled back, foam streaking his chin.

"Mutation illness," Ariga said, not breaking stride. "It doesn't come from the Beasts attacking. It comes from the air. From the water. Their bodies are changing faster than they can handle, and we have nothing to treat it."

Kael said nothing.

He forced himself to keep walking.

The second floor was worse in a different way.

It was quieter. The people here were too exhausted to cry. They waited, hollow-eyed, rationing nothing because there was nothing left to ration. Empty nutrient pods stacked in a corner. A dead man in the aisle that no one had moved yet. A mother shielding her child, watching Kael pass with the dull suspicion of someone who had been lied to for a month.

"How many?" Kael asked.

"Roughly three thousand civilians survived," Ariga answered. "That's it. Out of everything that was in this town. Three thousand, with no food, no medicine, and a dying shelter."

The third floor.

An armored hatch split the floor, guarded by two TPV soldiers with wind-up crossbows and a biometric lock that no longer worked. The door had been pried open by hand, using whatever tools the men could scavenge.

"The SDD sections," he said. "Comms, command, and what's left of our supply. We never got the power back down here, so we forced this door open manually with everything we could find. The backup cells aren't enough to run the communication systems."

Beyond the hatch, barely audible through the plating, a faint hum pulsed—then died, then pulsed again. The comms equipment was alive, but barely. Not enough to respond. Not enough to connect.

Iris spoke in Kael's mind.

"The hatch's authentication subsystem is offline. The terminal beyond it retains a partial power state—one communication line, weak, and unresponsive to manual input."

Ariga spoke, his eyes on the dead hatch.

"The soldiers have been attempting a connection to the Regional Command in Kyoto for weeks now, using every way they could think of. But—"

He shook his head.

"They are broadcasting across every standard band and receiving nothing," Iris finished in Kael's mind. "Their signal never leaves the shelter."

Kael said nothing.

"I can do it differently." Her voice was quieter now. "The terminal's communication line carries the shelter's registered signal data and its encryption keys. I can intercept that data as it cycles and transmit through my own system, using the shelter's identity but carrying the message over my own connection. Kyoto will receive it as a legitimate shelter status report. The soldiers will never know it wasn't routed through their own equipment."

She paused.

"This will be a covert transmission. No changes to the terminal's records, no visible activity on the shelter's communication system."

She waited.

"I will need your approval. This is not an authorized use of my systems."

Kael looked at the sleeping, dying floors above them.

"Do it," he said quietly.

He could feel her work—not through the terminal's circuits, but around them. Pulling the dormant signal data. Reading the routing tables. Building the report in the shelter's own voice, then slipping it out through her own unseen connection.

"Understood."

A single transmission, brief and clean, left the shelter minutes later.

Kael turned to Ariga.

"How long can we hold on?"

"With our remaining resources? Not even a single day," Ariga replied. "The number of injured and ill keeps climbing, and we have nothing left to treat them. We're running out of everything."

Before Kael could respond, a soldier rushed toward them.

"Captain! Movement detected along the perimeter!"

Outside, the mutated animals started attacking again.

---

Mutated raccoon dogs, wild boars, hares, weasels, badgers, martens, red squirrels, crows, and serows. A tide of twisted fur and muscle surged out of the dying afternoon light against the two defensive rings of High Mobility Vehicles.

The SDD defenders had already positioned the twelve-ton HMVs before Kael arrived. Two overlapping rings of armored vehicles formed a defensive funnel around the shelter's outer approach, their angled hulls creating choke points and narrow firing gaps. The arrangement forced approaching animals into exposed corridors where the defenders could concentrate their fire.

It was Ariga's men who had built the formation. The TPV line had used every surviving vehicle they could move, turning the wreckage and armored hulls into a makeshift fortress.

Kael studied the arrangement for only a moment.

He understood its purpose immediately.

Dusk approached, and the siege settled into a brutal rhythm.

The SDD defenders cycled through their firing positions behind the steel walls, using wind-up crossbows and air rifles in coordinated volleys. Their ammunition was limited, and every shot had to count.

Kael didn't remain behind the barricade.
