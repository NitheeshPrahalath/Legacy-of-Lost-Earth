There isn't a scientific survey that ranks **all animals of Mount Myōken (Hyōgo Prefecture)** by abundance. However, based on wildlife surveys from Hyōgo's low-to-mid elevation mountains (including the Myōken area), park records, and prefectural wildlife data, the following is a realistic ranking from **least commonly encountered to most commonly encountered**.

## Mammals (Least Common → Most Common)

1. Japanese Dormouse *(rare, nocturnal)*
2. Japanese Giant Flying Squirrel
3. Japanese Black Bear *(very rare in southern Hyōgo)*
4. Japanese Marten
5. Japanese Badger
6. Red Fox
7. Raccoon Dog (Tanuki)
8. Japanese Hare
9. Japanese Squirrel
10. Japanese Macaque
11. Sika Deer
12. Japanese Wild Boar *(most commonly detected large mammal)*

### If you're hiking Mount Myōken

The animals you're **most likely** to encounter are:

1. Japanese Wild Boar (tracks and rooting)
2. Sika Deer
3. Japanese Macaques
4. Brown-eared Bulbuls
5. Large-billed Crows
6. Japanese Tits
7. Tree Frogs
8. Cicadas
9. Butterflies
10. Squirrels

Predators such as black bears, hawk-eagles, and martens are present only in very low numbers or are extremely elusive, making sightings uncommon. Wildlife surveys in Hyōgo consistently report wild boar and deer as the dominant large mammals across the prefecture's forested mountains. ([kankyo.pref.hyogo.lg.jp][2])
